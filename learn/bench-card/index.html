Bench card

Set up

  • 330 °C for leaded solder, 360 °C for lead-free: the lowest that flows a joint in 2–3 s.
  • A chisel tip, 2–3 mm, clean and tinned: silver, not black.
  • 0.8 mm flux-core solder. Never plumbing solder or acid flux.
  • Glasses on, a fan pulling the smoke away, food off the bench.

Soldering tools and safety

The five-step joint

  1. Heat the pad and the lead together, about a second.
  2. Feed solder in on the far side, not onto the tip.
  3. Stop at a little volcano, and take the solder away.
  4. Lift the iron a moment later: 2–3 s in all.
  5. Keep still while it sets, then trim the lead.

How to solder

A good joint

  • Covers the whole pad, right to its edge.
  • Climbs the lead, whose outline still shows.
  • Concave, meeting the metal at a shallow angle.
  • Shiny, or satin with lead-free. No cracks, holes or lumps.
  • Touches nothing it shouldn't.

What a good joint looks like

Quick fixes

  • Dull and lumpy (cold): reheat with a touch of flux until it flows.
  • A ball that won't wet the pad: heat the pad too, with flux.
  • Bridge: drag it apart with a clean tip, or soak it up with braid.
  • Too little: reheat and add a little more.
  • Lifted pad: stop heating, and wire straight from the lead.

The joint doctor

Which way round

  • Electrolytic capacitor: stripe = −, the shorter lead.
  • Tantalum capacitor: stripe or + = positive.
  • Diode: band = cathode.
  • LED: long lead = +, flat side = cathode.
  • Chip: pin 1 by the notch, then counterclockwise, seen from above.

Mind polarity and orientation

Lowest parts first

  1. Wire links and jumpers that lie flat
  2. Resistors and diodes
  3. Chip sockets and pin headers
  4. Small capacitors and transistors
  5. Electrolytics, regulators and trimmers
  6. Connectors and anything tall

Which parts first

Before power

  1. Look: every joint, every gap, every part's polarity.
  2. Beep: every connection, and the neighbors that mustn't connect.
  3. Ohms from supply to ground, behind any protection diode: not near 0 Ω.
  4. Chips out, current limited: 50–100 mA, or 100 Ω 2 W in series.
  5. Volts at each chip socket. Then power off, chips in, power on.

Test and debug your board

Numbers

  • 2.54 mm (0.1 inch) between holes
  • 22–24 AWG solid wire for links and buses
  • 100 nF across every chip's power pins
  • 10–100 µF where power comes in
  • About 350 °C for 3 s: a typical limit on a part's lead
