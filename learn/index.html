Build along: a 555 LED blinker

The guides follow one small project all the way: a 555 timer flashing an LED about once a second from a 9 V battery. Build it as you read, and by the end you'll have used every technique in the series.

What you need

  • U1 NE555 timer, and an 8-pin DIP socket
  • R1, R2, R3 ¼ W resistors: 1 kΩ, 68 kΩ and 470 Ω
  • C1, C3 electrolytic capacitors, 16 V or more: 10 µF and 100 µF
  • C2, C4 ceramic capacitors: 10 nF and 100 nF
  • D1 a red LED, and D2 a 1N4001 diode
  • J1 a 2-way screw terminal (5.08 mm), or a 2-pin header (2.54 mm) for the stripboard version, and a 9 V battery clip
  • Perfboard of at least 20 × 15 holes: a 5 × 7 cm board has room
  • Bare tinned wire, and a little red and yellow solid-core wire
  • Optional: four M3 screws and standoffs

Where each step is

  1. Pick perfboard or stripboard
  2. Plan the layout
  3. Wire it
  4. Or lay it out on stripboard
  5. Get your tools ready
  6. Solder it, lowest parts first
  7. Test it and power it up

Numbers worth remembering

2.54 mmHole spacing (0.1 inch) on almost every board
330 / 360 °CA starting iron temperature for leaded / lead-free solder
2–3 sTo make a typical through-hole joint
0.8 mmThe all-round solder diameter for through-hole work
100 nFThe decoupling capacitor at every chip's power pins
22–24 AWGSolid wire for jumpers, buses and most connections
