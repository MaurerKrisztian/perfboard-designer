0–9

555 timer
A timer chip first sold in 1972 and still everywhere: with two resistors and a capacitor it blinks, beeps or keeps time.More in Build a 555 LED blinker

A

Anode
The positive end of a diode or LED, where current goes in. On an LED it's the longer lead.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Astable
A circuit that never settles but flips between high and low by itself, forever: how a 555 blinks an LED.More in Build a 555 LED blinker
AWG (American Wire Gauge)
Wire size by number: the smaller the number, the thicker the wire. 22 AWG is about 0.64 mm across, 30 AWG about 0.25 mm.More in Wire a perfboard

B

Breadboard
A plastic block of spring clips for trying a circuit out without soldering. Great for experiments, no home for a finished circuit.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?
Breadboard-style protoboard
A solderable board with its pads joined in a breadboard's pattern, so a breadboard circuit moves across part for part.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?
Bulk capacitor
A larger capacitor, say 10 to 100 µF, where power enters the board, steadying the supply for the whole circuit.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Bus wire
Bare tinned copper wire laid along a row of pads on the copper side, usually for the power rails.More in Wire a perfboard

C

Cathode
The negative end of a diode or LED, where current comes out: marked by the band on a diode and the flat side of an LED.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Cold joint
A dull, lumpy joint where the solder never properly melted onto the metal. It may work for now, but it's unreliable: reheat it.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)
Component side
The face of the board the parts sit on. The other face is the copper side.More in Wire a perfboard
Continuity mode
The multimeter setting that beeps when two points are connected: the quickest way to check a joint or a wire.More in Test and debug your board
Copper side
The face of the board with the copper pads or strips, where the leads poke through and get soldered. Also called the solder side.More in Wire a perfboard
Current limit
A bench power supply's ceiling on current. Set low for a first power-up, it stops a short from doing damage.More in Test and debug your board
Cut (track cut)
A break in a stripboard strip, made at a hole or between two holes, so one strip can carry two different connections.More in Stripboard (Veroboard) layout

D

Decoupling capacitor
A 100 nF capacitor right across a chip's power pins, supplying the sudden gulps of current the chip takes.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Desoldering braid
Fine copper braid, soaked in flux, that soaks up molten solder. Also called solder wick.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)
DIP (dual in-line package)
A chip with two rows of pins 0.1 inch apart, made to go through holes. The 555 is an 8-pin DIP.More in Plan a perfboard layout

E

Electrolytic capacitor
A capacitor with a large value in a small can. It's polarized: the stripe on the can marks the negative lead.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Enameled wire
Fine copper wire coated in varnish, as used in coils. Solderable kinds lose their enamel on a hot iron. Also called magnet wire.More in Wire a perfboard
ESD (electrostatic discharge)
A static spark, often too small to feel, that can damage CMOS chips and MOSFETs.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)

F

Flux
A cleaner that lets molten solder wet the metal instead of balling up. Electronics solder carries it in its core.More in Soldering tools and safety
FR-2 and FR-4
Board materials. FR-2 is cheap paper and resin, usually brown; FR-4 is stronger glass fiber in epoxy, usually green.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?

H

Heat-shrink
Plastic tubing that shrinks tight around a joint when heated, to insulate it. Slide it onto the wire before you solder.More in Wire a perfboard

J

Jumper
An insulated wire that hops over other connections, soldered through a hole at each end like a part.More in Wire a perfboard

L

Lead
A component's wire leg, the part that goes through the hole and gets soldered.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)
Lead-free solder
Solder without lead, such as SAC305. It melts at about 217 °C, needs a hotter iron, and its joints look duller.More in Soldering tools and safety
Link
On stripboard, a short wire joining two strips.More in Stripboard (Veroboard) layout

M

Mirror-image trap
Planning a board from above but soldering it from below, where left and right are swapped.More in Plan a perfboard layout

N

Net
Everything that's connected together: one connection, however many pins it reaches.More in Plan a perfboard layout

P

Pad
The copper ring around a hole, which the solder bonds to.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?
Perfboard
A board with a grid of holes, each with its own copper pad and none of them joined: you make every connection yourself.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?
Pin 1
The pin a chip's numbering starts from, by the notch or dot. The rest count counterclockwise, seen from above.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Pitch
The spacing between holes or pins: 2.54 mm (0.1 inch) on almost every prototyping board.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?
Plated-through hole
A hole lined with copper that joins the pads on both faces, so solder fills it and grips the lead along its length.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?
Polarized part
A part that only works one way round: diodes, LEDs, electrolytic capacitors and chips.More in Plan a perfboard layout

R

Rail
A supply line, positive or ground, running along the board so every part can reach it with a short hop.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Reverse-polarity diode
A diode in series with the power input, so a battery connected the wrong way round can't do any damage.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Rosin
The classic flux, made from pine resin. Its smoke irritates the airways, so keep your face out of it.More in Soldering tools and safety

S

Short circuit
A connection that shouldn't be there, usually between the supply and ground. Find it before you power up.More in Test and debug your board
Smoke test
Engineers' joke name for the first power-up. The checks before it make it a non-event.More in Test and debug your board
Socket
A holder soldered in a chip's place, so the chip never meets the iron and can be swapped in seconds.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Solder
A soft alloy, melted to join metals. For electronics: a thin wire with flux in its core, about 0.8 mm across.More in Soldering tools and safety
Solder bridge
Solder joining two pads. Made on purpose it's a connection; made by accident it's a short.More in Wire a perfboard
Solder side
Another name for the copper side, where you solder.More in Plan a perfboard layout
Spot-face cutter
A hand tool with a cutting tip, twisted in a stripboard hole to cut the strip there.More in Stripboard (Veroboard) layout
Strain relief
Anchoring a wire before its joint, so a tug pulls on the board and not on the solder.More in Wire a perfboard
Stripboard
A board whose copper runs in strips, each joining a whole row of holes until you cut it.More in Stripboard (Veroboard) layout
Surface-mount (SMD)
Parts that sit on pads on top of a board instead of going through holes. On perfboard they go on adapter boards.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?

T

Through-hole
Parts whose leads go through holes in the board and are soldered on the other side: everything in these guides.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)
Tinning
Coating a tip, wire or pad with a thin layer of solder, so it takes heat and joins quickly.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)

V

Veroboard
The best-known brand of stripboard, often used as a name for any stripboard.More in Perfboard, stripboard or breadboard?

W

Wetting
Molten solder spreading over clean, hot metal and bonding with it, meeting it at a shallow angle: the sign of a good joint.More in How to solder (and fix bad joints)
Wire-wrap wire
Thin (30 AWG) insulated wire for dense signal wiring, run point to point on the copper side. Also called Kynar wire.More in Wire a perfboard
